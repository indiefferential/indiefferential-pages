Suno Studio Launches 2.0 With Enhanced Features

By Mark Hale
August 16, 2026

AI music company Suno announced the launch of Studio 2.0, a significant update to its browser-based digital audio workstation (DAW). The new version includes features such as MIDI support, a beta AI chat bar, and advanced stem separation, along with new audio effects and automations.

Suno emphasizes that “Grammy-winning artists” have used Suno Studio in the past to create chart-topping hits. However, the company has also implemented stricter download limits for its free users. Free users will be limited to seven downloads per lifetime account, while Pro subscribers (at $8–$10 a month) and Premier subscribers (at $24–$30 a month) are restricted to 20 and 60 monthly downloads, respectively.

Despite these changes, Suno Studio, available only to its Premier subscribers, will have no download limits. This move reflects Suno’s commitment to supporting musicians who use the platform for professional purposes.

Henry Phipps, Suno’s Studio Product Manager, highlighted in a blog post that “perhaps our biggest takeaway from spending time with artists is their deep appreciation for the instruments and tools that have earned a place in their studios.” He emphasized the importance of maintaining access to traditional music foundations like guitars, drum machines, synths, effects, and step sequencers within the next generation of music software.

Suno Studio 2.0’s most requested addition, MIDI support, allows users to import, record, and edit MIDI on the timeline. In a unique feature, MIDI clips can also be used as prompts for new audio generations generated by Studio. The chat bar, launching in beta, lets users interact with Suno “like you would any other collaborator,” suggesting it can create instruments and vocals, build custom plugins, or even tidy up sessions.

Phipps noted that text prompts are useful for finding inspiration but emphasized that they are no substitute for playing a part on a MIDI keyboard or recording with a microphone. The chat feature is described as a tool to help musicians collaborate more effectively within the platform.

Mark Hale

Mark Hale is a writer, editor, and long-time cultural commentator specialising in guitars, music technology, and the quietly absurd world that surrounds them. He writes about instruments the way other people write about cars: with affection, irritation, and an unwavering belief that function matters more than mythology. Raised in a household where music was not a hobby but a constant presence, Hale grew up surrounded by vinyl records, half-functional amplifiers, and instruments that were always slightly out of tune. From an early age, he learned that guitars had personalities. Some were dependable. Some were brilliant but infuriating. Some looked magnificent and behaved like absolute idiots. This belief—that tools reveal character—has stayed with him and underpins his entire approach to writing. Before becoming a full-time writer, Hale spent years embedded in the unglamorous machinery of the music world. He worked in guitar shops where marketing promises collided daily with reality, learning very quickly which instruments survived real players and which existed purely for catalog photography. He loaded vans, wired pedalboards minutes before soundcheck, repaired cables with borrowed soldering irons, and watched outstanding musicians coax magic from battered gear while others struggled heroically with instruments that cost more than a small car.
