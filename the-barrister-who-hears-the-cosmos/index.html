The Barrister Who Hears the Cosmos

Few artists arrive with a résumé like this one. Anthony John Sissian is an Australian polymath, tech entrepreneur and commercial litigator whose music sits at an unusual meeting point of structural logic and metaphysical longing. His background reads like several careers at once. He trained at NIDA in music theatre, became a Solicitor and Barrister of the High Court of Australia, led Sissian & Associates, and served as Head of Litigation Oceania for Ernst & Young. He also founded Procon Telematics and developed proprietary AI data patents. Through all of it, music remained a serious pursuit. His compositions span classical writing, experimental sound design and dramatic performance. In 2015, his string octet The Whistler was premiered by the Sydney Lawyers Orchestra, programmed alongside works by Mozart, Handel and Telemann. In April 2026, Mexico's Cosmonauta Radio published a review of his symphonic work that compared one track to the Sistine Chapel of music, praising its grandeur and the high bar it sets within its genre. Sissian works entirely independently through his imprint Aurora Sentient, without traditional commercial machinery, and the streaming response has been notable for a cold start. His progressive electronic anthem “Break the Circuit” climbed to number 19 on the Groover charts, while the high-concept single “I Found Home in Thee” reached number 221 globally among thousands of active tracks, matching a peak of number six regionally. Then came a turning point. On January 1, 2026, after returning from the Monroe Institute's Gateway Voyage in Virginia, Sissian experienced what he regards as a creative awakening, with fully formed music and lyrics arriving unbidden. The outcome was extraordinary in sheer scale: four complete concept albums written, arranged, recorded and produced entirely alone in six months. Their titles are YOU thee ME, HOME, Verus Orion and Barbault Quadrature. Sissian does not perform live or appear on camera. Instead, he lets the voice, the words and what he calls structural frequencies stand as an unfiltered canvas. It is an unusually pure form of anonymity, one that places all of the attention on the work itself. The sheer range of the biography makes the work more intriguing rather than less. Classical composition, experimental sound design and theatrical performance all feed into the same body of music, and the discipline of a legal and technological career appears to inform its structure. The result is a catalog that treats composition as a form of systems thinking, with emotion and logic working side by side. For listeners drawn to ambitious electronic and symphonic music with philosophical depth, his catalog offers a rich and unconventional world to explore.
