The Millionaire Who Came Back Wiser

Some artists spend decades learning what their first album was really about. Erwin Radjinder, the Dutch singer-songwriter and producer of Dutch-Hindustani descent, returns with a new chapter that looks back as much as forward. His focus track, “It's Me (Then & Now),” opens the five-song EP Now, scheduled for release in September 2026, and it is a melancholic, orchestral power-pop ballad about identity, the roles taken on through life, and the need to still be seen underneath them. The song captures a universal experience with gentle precision. Over time, people become parents, professionals and public versions of themselves, and the person beneath can feel hard to locate. By treating that question inside an orchestral ballad, Radjinder gives it dramatic weight without losing intimacy. The EP explores identity, ambition, love and the search for meaning from different angles, suggesting a project built as a set of connected reflections. His musical language is rooted in classic pop and rock. Beatlesque melodies and Beach Boys-inspired harmonies meet contemporary production, and his songs combine introspection, irony and melodic craftsmanship. That mix of warmth and wit is a distinctive signature, and it keeps even serious themes from becoming heavy. Radjinder's history gives the new material added resonance. He debuted in 2002 with the concept album Just Another Millionaire, an exploration of meaning and the search for happiness in a world obsessed with fame. The single “Nice & Easy” reached the Dutch Mega Top 100 in 2003. After a remastered and re-edited reissue of that album and a series of reimagined singles, 2026 marks a new chapter built from entirely new material. The singles “(Only A) Businessman” and “Beautiful Beautiful Girl” lead up to the EP. There is a pleasing symmetry in this arc. An album that questioned the pursuit of fame in 2002 now gives way to music that questions the roles people play, and the connection suggests a consistent artistic concern across more than two decades. “It's Me (Then & Now)” sounds like the work of a songwriter who has kept his curiosity intact. Craft is the quiet constant. The melodies are built with care, the harmonies lean toward the sunlit tradition of classic pop, and the irony in the writing prevents sentiment from turning syrupy. It is a combination that has served the songwriter since his debut, and it gives the new EP a familiar warmth even as its subject matter turns more reflective. For listeners who love melodic pop with orchestral sweep and thoughtful lyrics, Radjinder offers a welcome and reflective return.
