A conversation with MILYAM

What is a typical day in your life as an artist?

"It's far less glamorous than people think and demands absolute discipline. I don't just write songs; I run MILYAM EMPIRE. Right now, for instance, I'm in the Dominican Republic shooting a music video, so my days are a mix of directing on set, juggling calls across time zones, and writing new songs. My team and I are deeply involved in everything — from creative direction to negotiating our own PR deals. A typical day comes down to building the empire, piece by piece."

How would you define success? What is your goal?

"At this moment, I am incredibly proud that 'Intimacy' is a Semi-Finalist in the R&B/Soul category of Unsigned Only 2026. It's a real milestone. But ultimately, success is absolute creative and financial autonomy on a global scale. My goal isn't just to have a fleeting hit; it's to establish MILYAM EMPIRE as a powerhouse label, retain 100% ownership of both my masters and my publishing, and bring my cinematic vision to the world's biggest stages. I want to reach a level of success and influence where my family — who make up my core team — will have complete freedom, and where my artistic choices are never compromised by industry pressure."

You've created your own term, "Atmospheric Sound." Why was it important to define your sound on your own terms?

"Because the industry constantly tries to put you in neat little boxes. I saw generic labels like 'indie-pop' being thrown around, and it just didn't capture the depth of what I do. My music is cinematic; it's left-field Art Rap and Dark R&B. It lives on the line between shadow and light, vulnerability and strength, and it requires a specific emotional register. I had to name it myself to protect the DNA of my brand and tell curators exactly what universe they are stepping into."

"Intimacy" is now in rotation on Amazing Radio in both the US and UK. What do you think international audiences are connecting with in your music?

"I think people are starved for genuine tension and vulnerability. 'Intimacy' is a deeply personal song; it offers a very specific late-night atmosphere. I believe listeners in both the US and the UK resonate with that exact contrast — raw human vulnerability set inside a soundscape built on a cinematic scale. My songs are stories I've lived, and a direct conversation with the listener."

AI is changing the creative process and challenging ideas of originality. What do you think a human artist can offer that AI never will?

"Only a human can make a sound that's genuinely emotive. AI assembles what already exists; a human artist takes risks and makes mistakes, and it's precisely out of that risk that something new is born. They can push music in new directions, blending genres based on their authentic vision and personal experiences. This stems from how they perceive the world and the genuine emotions they've lived through. I think this breath of fresh air — this visceral sound that actually cuts through — is exactly what new artists bring to the industry."

You've chosen to remain independent. What has independence given you creatively, and what has it made more difficult?

"Creatively, it has given me uncompromising aesthetic discipline. Nobody tells me to make a track 'more commercial' or interferes with my visual storytelling. The difficulty? You are the engine. You handle logistics, funding, international negotiations — and your own burnout. But I am incredibly lucky to have a strong team. My mom is the co-creator of our conceptual brand, MILYAM EMPIRE; she handles the management and styling of the entire project. And my brother is a highly talented photographer and videographer, with a degree in journalism. He has amazing taste and vision, and he shot many of our covers and promo photos. Ultimately, retaining full ownership of my masters, my publishing, and my vision makes every sleepless night completely worth it."

In an era where artists are expected to constantly share their lives online, do you think mystery still has a place in music?

"Absolutely. In fact, mystery is the ultimate luxury right now. I take a minimalist approach to my public presence: I give the audience enough to enter my universe, but I leave room for them to project their own emotions onto the music. You don't need to overshare every second of your day to build a profound connection. The music itself is the most intimate space I can offer."
