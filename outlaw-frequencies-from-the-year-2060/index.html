Outlaw Frequencies From the Year 2060

Pop music rarely arrives with a mission statement and a time machine. L'VON does. The genre-defying cyberpunk rebel, who uses they/them pronouns and describes themself as coming from the year 2060, fuses hypnotic melodies with immersive EDM-pop soundscapes, and makes music for the ladies, gays and theys leaving their mark on the world. Born outside Atlanta and now based in Baltimore, they bring a distinct voice to a crowded genre. Their sophomore EP, BORN A SINNER, details the experience of growing up Black, queer and gender-nonconforming in rural Georgia. The writing is described as raw and loud, built for EDM and pop lovers alike, and the combination of danceable energy and personal testimony gives the project its force. This is music made for movement, but it also has something to say. The lead single, “Outlaw,” is a western-infused anthem about carving a path when every road was built by someone else, for someone else. It is a vivid metaphor, and it sets cowboy imagery against electronic production to create a sound that feels both familiar and unmistakably futuristic. The song speaks directly to the LGBTQIA+ community and to people of color around the world, and to anyone who has dared to be themselves when the world demands a narrow box. Influences help to locate the sound. L'VON cites Kesha, Tove Lo, MNEK and Troye Sivan, artists who combine pop hooks with emotional openness and a willingness to be bold. From that lineage, L'VON is working toward a lane of their own in EDM and pop spaces. The artist's framing, with its talk of a live frequency and an expanding universe, gives the project a sense of world-building. It treats identity as both a theme and a design principle, with a coherent aesthetic that connects the storytelling, the production and the persona. Just as importantly, it never loses sight of the dance floor. “Outlaw” and BORN A SINNER suggest an artist who understands that visibility and celebration can happen together. The storytelling gives the glitter weight. Growing up Black, queer and gender-nonconforming in rural Georgia is the raw material of BORN A SINNER, and the project transforms that experience into something loud, communal and defiantly joyful rather than solemn. It is a reminder that pop can carry testimony without losing its shine, and that a dance floor can be a place where a person is finally seen. For listeners who want electronic pop with heart, rebellion and a clear point of view, L'VON offers a thrilling invitation.
